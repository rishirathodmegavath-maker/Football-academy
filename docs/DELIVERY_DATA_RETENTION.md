# Delivery-data retention: recommendation for approval

Status: **partly superseded.** The owner decided that delivery history is kept for 12 months, and that part is implemented: see `DELIVERY_RETENTION.md`. The questions that decision does not settle (raw webhook bodies, audit, archives, erasure, finance periods) remain open as written below. The original status line read: **proposal. Nothing here is implemented, and no data is deleted by this change.** The numbers marked "proposed" are options for the owner (and, where personal data or accounting records are involved, for their legal and accounting advisers). They are not facts about any law.

Written for: the application owner, who decides how long messaging history is kept.

## 1. What grows, and how fast

Measured on this code with a synthetic history (100,000 delivered WhatsApp jobs, each with one provider attempt, plus 20,000 webhook events, with all eight indexes present, `evidence/p7-growth-measure.json`): **about 1.4 KB per delivered job** in the database file. Row payloads are small (a job is about 250 bytes); most of the size is the indexes that make the lists and the worker fast.

Projection (arithmetic on the measurement, not a forecast): an academy of 500 children with 650 guardians that sends each guardian a weekly plan (52), a monthly report (12) and about 30 announcement or reply messages a year creates roughly 100 jobs per guardian: **65,000 jobs a year, about 90 MB a year** in `delivery_jobs`, `delivery_attempts` and `communication_events`. Ten such academies for five years is about 4.5 GB. SQLite handles that without trouble, and the paged list and the indexes keep reads fast whatever the size (`evidence/p7-jobs-perf.json`: every page 0.1-2.7 ms at 150,000 jobs). The reasons to decide a policy are **privacy and backup size**, not speed.

## 2. What must not be deleted by a retention job

- **Financial records** (`academy_finance_records`, `academy_finance_history`, invoices, payments, receipts) and their audit rows. Accounting and tax retention periods are set by each country and are usually years; the owner's accountant must state the period for each country served. A retention job must never touch these tables.
- **`email_suppressions`.** Removing a bounced or complaining address would let the system mail it again.
- **Report and update approval history** that backs a report a parent has received. It is the evidence of what was approved and by whom.
- **Anything in a state that can still change** (a job that is `QUEUED` or claimed, an unprocessed event, an unresolved reminder).
- **Rows that provide duplicate protection while a duplicate can still arrive.** `delivery_jobs.providerId` is unique so that a webhook replayed by the provider cannot be applied twice; deleting the job removes that protection, so a job must outlive the provider's retry window for its status webhooks (the provider documents it; it is days, not months).

## 3. Recommendation

| Class                             | Records                                                                                                                                                                                      | Proposed treatment                                                                                                                                                                     | Proposed period (options for the owner)                                                                                                                                                             |
| --------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Keep**                          | financial records and their history; report and update approval history; email suppressions; academy, membership and consent records; the current consent and opt-out state of each guardian | no automatic deletion                                                                                                                                                                  | as long as the academy account exists, and after closure as the accountant and the privacy adviser require                                                                                          |
| **Archive, then delete**          | `delivery_jobs` and `delivery_attempts` in a final state (`DELIVERED`, `READ`, `FAILED`, `CANCELLED`, expired)                                                                               | export to a compressed, checksummed file per academy and month, kept with the backups, then delete from the live database in small batches                                             | **Option A: 12 months live. Option B: 24 months live. Option C: 6 months live.** Longer live retention helps support ("did the parent get the March report?") and costs about 90 MB per 65,000 jobs |
| **Delete after a short time**     | `communication_events` that are processed                                                                                                                                                    | delete the stored raw body (phone number, reply text) first and keep a stub (id, time, outcome) for the audit; the content of a guardian's reply is already stored in the conversation | **30-90 days** for the raw body; stub as long as the job it describes                                                                                                                               |
| **Delete after a short time**     | `*_submissions` idempotency receipts                                                                                                                                                         | delete once a request id can no longer be retried by a client                                                                                                                          | **30 days** is far beyond any client retry                                                                                                                                                          |
| **Archive, then delete (slowly)** | `workspace_audit`                                                                                                                                                                            | the audit is the accountability record; archive by year, keep the archive, keep the last 24 months live                                                                                | keep **at least as long as the financial records of the same academy** (the audit explains them)                                                                                                    |
| **Already deleted**               | finished email and provider events                                                                                                                                                           | no change                                                                                                                                                                              | 90 days (existing)                                                                                                                                                                                  |
| **Delete on schedule**            | expired sessions, expired counters                                                                                                                                                           | verify and, if needed, add a purge of expired `session` rows                                                                                                                           | days                                                                                                                                                                                                |

Why archive first: a deletion is not recoverable from the live database, and the backups are the only other copy. An archive with a checksum that is verified before the delete means a support or legal question years later can still be answered, and the owner can later decide to destroy the archives on their own schedule.

Recovery and troubleshooting needs, and how this satisfies them: the live window (6-24 months) covers "did this message go out?" questions; the backups (hourly 24, daily 30, monthly 12 by default, `docs/BACKUP_AND_RECOVERY.md`) still contain deleted rows for up to a year, which is itself a privacy point to state in the privacy notice; the archive covers later questions.

## 4. Privacy, audit and financial implications

- **Children's and guardians' data.** A job row ties a named guardian to a named child and to what was sent. The longer it is kept the larger the exposure in a breach and the longer a deletion or access request must reach. Data protection law in the academy's country decides what is "necessary"; the application stores no reason for keeping it beyond operations. **Ask the privacy adviser for the period per country**; if the academies are in several countries the shortest practical period that meets operations is the safe default.
- **Right to erase.** A retention job is not an erasure feature. A guardian's request to be erased (where the law gives it) needs a separate, audited action that removes or anonymises that guardian's contact, jobs and events and leaves financial records intact. It is not built; list it as a decision (D5).
- **Audit.** Deleting delivery history must itself be audited: one summary `workspace_audit` row per run ("removed N jobs older than D days, archive X, checksum Y").
- **Finance.** No retention job touches money tables (section 2). Receipts and invoices remain regenerable from the kept records.

## 5. How it would be implemented (after approval)

1. **Dry run first.** A report-only mode prints, per academy and table, how many rows are past the period and how many bytes they hold. No deletion. The owner reads it for a month.
2. **Archive step.** A script (same style as `scripts/backup-database.mjs`) writes JSON Lines per academy and month, compresses it, writes a SHA-256, **reads the file back and compares counts and checksum**, and only then reports success.
3. **Delete step.** In the worker, once a day, in batches of a few thousand rows inside a transaction, only rows whose archive is verified, only final states, only older than the approved period, with a hard per-run cap and a stop flag. `PRAGMA wal_checkpoint` and an occasional `VACUUM` (outside business hours) return the space.
4. **Kill-switch and visibility.** `SPORTOZEN_RETENTION=off|report|on`, defaulting to `off`. The ops check (`scripts/ops-check.mjs`) gains a warning when the delivery tables grow beyond a configured size with retention off.
5. **Tests.** The retention job must prove: it never selects a row in a non-final state; never touches a financial table or the suppression list (a test that lists the tables it may write to); archive checksum mismatch aborts the delete; a re-run is idempotent; a job referenced by an unexpired provider receipt window stays; the paged list and the announcement list still work after the oldest rows are gone; restore of a backup taken before a purge still opens. Mutation checks on each guard.
6. **Rollback.** Turn the switch off. Deleted rows come back only from the archive or a backup, which is why step 2 comes first.

## 6. Decisions needed from the owner

- **D1** Live retention for delivery jobs and attempts: 6, 12 or 24 months (or another figure).
- **D2** Raw inbound webhook bodies: 30, 60 or 90 days.
- **D3** Audit rows: live period, and the archive period (suggested: not shorter than the financial records).
- **D4** Archives: where they are kept, who can read them, when they are destroyed.
- **D5** Whether a guardian-erasure feature is wanted, and in which countries it is legally required.
- **D6** The financial-record period per country (from the accountant), only to be recorded; no automation proposed.

Until these are decided the system keeps everything, which is the safe choice for a pilot.

## Documented interpretations and distinctions

### 5. Two-Touch Juggling Circle

* **Interpretation:** 'Two-touch' is read as: each player takes at most two touches (a control and a pass) before the ball moves on. It is not a juggling count.

### 7. Dynamic Sprint & Acceleration

* **Interpretation:** The approved '50% to 100%' is read as an effort build-up within each run (easy to full speed). Sportozen sets no number of runs or distances; the coach decides.

### 8. Wall-Kicks / Wall Passing

* **Interpretation:** 'Wall-Kicks' and 'Wall Passing' are two names for the same drill: passing against a rebound surface. It is an individual technical drill, not a partner drill.
* **Distinct from drill 22:** Drill 8 uses a passive rebound surface and the player stays largely in place to train touch and accuracy. Drill 22 uses a teammate as the 'wall' and trains movement and timing after the pass.
* **Distinct from drill 16:** Drill 16 is a three-player shape and support practice; Drill 8 is one player against a surface.

### 9. Stair Steppers on the Ball

* **Interpretation:** 'Stair stepper' is read as alternating light sole touches on top of the ball in a quick, stepping rhythm.

### 13. Blindfold Soccer Movements

* **Interpretation:** Redesigned as a controlled, supervised proprioception activity: eyes closed only, no physical blindfold by default, stationary or slow controlled movements only, in individual marked lanes. Nothing is claimed about injury prevention or rehabilitation.
* **Minimum age:** 12 (internal product policy; not a football association rule).

### 16. Three-Player Passing Triangles

* **Distinct from drill 22:** Drill 16 trains group shape and support angles in a rotating triangle of three. Drill 22 isolates the one-two between two players and progresses it with movement.
* **Distinct from drill 8:** Drill 8 is one player against a rebound surface; Drill 16 always involves three teammates.

### 18. The Time Glass Passing Drill

* **Interpretation:** Sportozen's defined interpretation, not an established historical drill: an hourglass-shaped passing layout (two triangles joined at a narrow waist) run in timed rounds. The name was not found as a recognised drill in the coaching sources searched.

### 22. Wall Pass (One-Two) Progression

* **Distinct from drill 8:** Drill 8 uses a passive rebound surface and trains first touch and accuracy in place. Drill 22 uses a teammate as the wall and trains movement and timing around the pass.
* **Distinct from drill 16:** Drill 16 works on a three-player shape and support angles. Drill 22 isolates the pair's one-two and builds it up step by step.

### 24. Long-Ball Aerial Control

* **Interpretation:** Chest and foot control only. No heading, not even as an alternative; the practice is deliberately limited to the chest and foot.

### 25. 4v2 Rondos

* **Distinct from drill 26:** Drill 25 is four outside players against two inside defenders with the emphasis on transition. Drill 26 is a positional three-against-one with one neutral support player.
* **Distinct from drill 27:** Drill 27 is a larger circle with rotating roles and decision-making. Drill 25 is a small square with four outside players.

### 26. 3v1 + 1 Rondo

* **Interpretation:** The '+1' is one neutral supporting player who always plays with the team in possession. The neutral is shown as 'N' in the diagram.
* **Distinct from drill 25:** Drill 25 uses four outside players against two defenders. Drill 26 is a positional 3v1 with a single neutral support player to recycle possession.
* **Distinct from drill 30:** Drill 30 uses floaters on a bigger grid with teams. Drill 26 is a small rondo with one defender and one neutral.

### 27. Circle Possession

* **Interpretation:** 'Responsibility' is read as each player's job within the circle: offering a clear angle, choosing the pass and stepping in as defender when it is their turn.
* **Distinct from drill 25:** Drill 25 is a small square with four players outside. Drill 27 is a larger circle with more players and rotating defenders, to emphasise decisions and individual responsibility.

### 28. Grid Keepaway

* **Distinct from drill 29:** Drill 28 is a single grid focused on team shape. Drill 29 splits the grid into two sections and rewards penetration to a target.
* **Distinct from drill 30:** Drill 30 adds neutral floaters; Drill 28 has no neutral players.

### 29. Two-Section Keepaway

* **Distinct from drill 28:** Drill 28 is one grid about team shape. Drill 29 splits the grid and rewards a pass through to a target, so it trains penetration.

### 30. Floater Grid Possession

* **Distinct from drill 26:** Drill 26 is a small rondo with one defender and a single neutral. Drill 30 has two full teams and floaters in a bigger grid.
* **Distinct from drill 28:** Drill 28 has no neutral players at all.

### 31. 2v2 + 2 Small-Sided Possession

* **Interpretation:** The '+2' is two neutral supporting players who always play with the team in possession. They are shown as 'N' in the diagram.
* **Distinct from drill 25:** Drill 25 is four outside players against two defenders. Drill 31 is a 2v2 game with two neutrals who help whoever has the ball.

### 32. Four-Team Four-Goal Game

* **Interpretation:** Four teams of equal size, each defending its own goal, with the ball played at any other team's goal.

### 33. Numbers Game

* **Interpretation:** 'Transition chaos' is controlled: the coach calls numbers at set moments so extra players join or leave, and the group has to recognise and use the overload quickly.

### 34. Hurdle Shooting

* **Interpretation:** Redesigned for safety: hurdles are low, soft or collapsible (or flat markers for younger players), with a clear landing area. The shot is taken only after balance is regained, never in the air.

### 37. Corner-Restricted 2v2

* **Interpretation:** A 2v2 played in a small marked zone in the corner of the pitch, with a goal to attack.

### 38. World Cup Shooting Game

* **Interpretation:** A World Cup themed competition played in rounds. To keep everyone involved, scoring is points-based and rounds advance the leaders without leaving anyone standing idle for long.

### 39. Midfield Goals Transition

* **Interpretation:** Small goals are placed on the halfway line so teams attack towards the middle of the pitch, which encourages spreading wide and quick strikes after a turnover.

### 41. 1v1 into 2v2 and 3v3 Progression

* **Interpretation:** 'Recovery' means defensive recovery: getting back into position, covering teammates and regaining shape as numbers grow. It does not mean physical rest.

### 42. 2v1 Defending When Outnumbered

* **Distinct from drill 44:** Drill 42 is one defender delaying two attackers. Drill 44 is a larger block-organisation game with several goals.

### 43. 3-Team Transition Game (2v4 Intense Pressing)

* **Interpretation:** '2v4' is read as two pressers against four players in possession, with a third team resting. The three-team rotation builds recovery into the practice.

### 44. Four-Goal Number-Down

* **Interpretation:** Two teams, four goals (two for each team to defend). One team is a player short and organises as a compact block.
* **Distinct from drill 42:** Drill 42 is one defender delaying two attackers. Drill 44 trains a whole team's block organisation in a larger game.
* **Distinct from drill 32:** Drill 32 has four teams. Drill 44 has two teams, four goals and an intentional numerical disadvantage.

### 45. Breaking the Block

* **Interpretation:** Kept under Defending & Pressing as approved. The practice trains both roles: the block holds its shape, and the attackers work to dislodge it.

### 46. Sprinting Circuit with Ball

* **Interpretation:** Sportozen's content describes short, fast efforts with the ball and full rest. It makes no physiological claims, and the number of efforts, distances and rest are set by the coach.

### 47. Center Circle Transition Sprints

* **Interpretation:** 'Recovery runs' are defensive recovery runs back to a position after possession is lost, not physical rest.

### 49. Heading Control & Clearance

* **Interpretation:** Stored as DRAFT. The drill is split into a non-heading control and clearance part (written below) and a heading part that is not authored and cannot be enabled until the relevant country's guidance has been verified against primary sources. India is the initial jurisdiction and no Indian guidance has been verified.
* **Heading component:** NOT_AUTHORED_PENDING_VERIFICATION. No heading instruction, age limit or repetition limit has been written. It may only be authored and enabled after primary-source verification for the academy's country.

### 50. Field Thirds Shape & Switching

* **Interpretation:** A near full-team practice with continuous movement of the team's shape through the thirds of the pitch. 'Endurance' comes from continuous play; no physiological claims are made.

## Safety content by drill

**1. Dynamic Mobility Circuit**

* Players move within their own comfortable range; nobody is pushed to stretch further.
* Check that the surface is dry, flat and free of obstacles, and that footwear suits it.
* Any player may stop at once without giving a reason; the coach stops the drill if control or spacing is lost.

**2. 4-Grid Warm-Up**

* Keep each group inside its own grid and avoid crossing between grids.
* Keep spare balls outside the playing area so nobody steps on one.
* Allow water breaks, and adapt or stop the activity for any player who looks unwell or tired.

**3. Ball-Feel Coordination**

* Players stay in their own space and keep an eye on others near them.
* Check that the surface is dry, flat and free of obstacles, and that footwear suits it.
* Keep spare balls outside the playing area so nobody steps on one.

**4. Tag Game with Balls**

* Tags are a light touch of the hand on the back or shoulder only: no pushing, pulling or grabbing.
* Keep balls under control so players do not collide; stop and reset if the grid becomes crowded.
* Keep spare balls outside the playing area so nobody steps on one.

**5. Two-Touch Juggling Circle**

* Serve low and gentle, especially for younger players, so the ball never arrives at the face.
* Use chest, thigh or foot for control; this drill does not use the head.
* Keep spare balls outside the playing area so nobody steps on one.

**6. Shadow Play**

* Keep a gap between partners so there is no contact, and keep pairs well apart.
* Give each pair or group clear space so paths do not cross; stop and reset if they do.
* Check that the surface is dry, flat and free of obstacles, and that footwear suits it.

**7. Dynamic Sprint & Acceleration**

* Only one player runs in a lane at a time, and the run-off area is kept clear.
* Players warm up before the faster runs; the coach decides the number of runs and rest by watching technique and fatigue.
* Check that the surface is dry, flat and free of obstacles, and that footwear suits it.
* Allow water breaks, and adapt or stop the activity for any player who looks unwell or tired.

**8. Wall-Kicks / Wall Passing**

* The wall must be smooth and safe, with no glass, sharp edges or hazards behind the ball.
* Keep lanes clear so rebounds do not hit another player; stop if a ball strays.
* Keep spare balls outside the playing area so nobody steps on one.

**9. Stair Steppers on the Ball**

* Players stay in their own space; keep clear of others.
* Rest between sequences if tired or off balance.
* Check that the surface is dry, flat and free of obstacles, and that footwear suits it.

**10. Coned Dribbling Slalom**

* Only one player dribbles in a line at a time and the next waits behind.
* Check that the surface is dry, flat and free of obstacles, and that footwear suits it.
* Keep spare balls outside the playing area so nobody steps on one.
